AutoMinder MVP Plan

What ships to the Play Store, in what order, and what has to be true before any of it goes out.

2026-08-16 com.autominder.app v1.0 · feature-frozen Billing deadline · 15 days

Thesis

AutoMinder is the app that tells you what your car needs before it costs you money.

The market is full of maintenance loggers. AutoMinder does not win on logging. It wins on one promise: the reminder arrives on time, and everything it says is true.

That leaves exactly three things worth being best at — the reminder fires correctly, nothing shown is invented, and logging costs almost nothing. Charts, OCR, analytics and silhouettes are decoration on top of those three, and none of them ship until all three are proven.

The finding that reorders this plan

Revised on second audit · highest risk

The reminder engine cannot keep the promise — and it has no tests.

The first version of this plan said the engine was untested. True, but too generous. This is the entire scheduling strategy:

PeriodicWorkRequestBuilder<ReminderCheckWorker>(6, TimeUnit.HOURS).build() WorkManager.getInstance(context) .enqueueUniquePeriodicWork(NAME, ExistingPeriodicWorkPolicy.KEEP, request)

PeriodicWorkRequest is best-effort. It is deferred by Doze, by App Standby, and by the OEM process killers on Xiaomi, Huawei, Oppo, Vivo, Samsung and Realme — which is most of the South Asian market this product targets. On those phones the reminder never arrives, and nothing tells the user.

There is no AlarmManager for date-critical items like insurance expiry, no exact-alarm permission declared, and zero battery-optimisation handling anywhere in the codebase. KEEP also means a future schedule change never reaches existing installs.

On top of that, all four workers have no tests between them while 70 tests pass elsewhere.

A maintenance app whose reminder silently fails is worse than no app — the user believes they are covered, and finds out when something breaks. That is the exact harm this product exists to prevent.

Make the reminder engine trustworthy

The highest-value work in this plan, and larger than first scoped. This is the product; everything else is packaging.

  • Fix the architecture — AlarmManager for date-critical items, exact-alarm permission declared, a battery-optimisation exemption asked once in context, POLICY.UPDATE so schedule changes reach existing installs, rescheduling on boot, update and timezone change
  • Prove it — worker selection and cooldowns, boot rescheduling, an instrumented test that the notification posts and deep-links correctly, and a device pass through reboot, force-stop, Doze and one aggressive-OEM handset
  • Be honest when we cannot deliver — if notifications are off or the app is battery-restricted, Home says so. Never let the user believe they are covered when they are not.

Standing rules

—No fabricated data, ever. An absent value and a zero value are different facts.
—No advertised capability that does not exist — in the UI, the store listing, or the locale filters.
—Status is never carried by colour alone, and never by shape.
—Every bug fix gets a regression test. “Compiles” is not “works”.
—State plainly what was not verified.
—A spec that cites external research gets checked before anything is built on it. One handoff document attributed a bento-grid pattern to Mobbin; live Mobbin research showed the opposite.
