# AutoMinder US Validation Scripts (plain English, no coaching)

## A. 20-Driver Beta Script (~25 min per session)
Recruit: ordinary Android drivers, 3+ states, mix of dealer/independent-shop customers, newer + older cars, low and high car confidence. Do not recruit mechanics or app developers.

Setup: their own phone, install link, screen share or sit beside them. Say only what is in quotes. Record times and where they hesitate.

1. "Install the app and set it up for your real car. Talk out loud while you do it."
   - Record: time to first verdict, any field they skip, any confusion word-for-word.
2. "What is the app telling you about your car right now? In your own words."
   - Pass = they name the verdict and one next action without prompting.
3. "Here's a repair estimate." (hand them the sample quote card, or ask them to use a real one with personal info covered) "Use the app to look it over before you'd approve it."
   - Record: do they find Counter Mode unaided? Time to triage. Which labels they misread.
4. "What would you actually say to the mechanic based on this?"
   - Pass = they use or paraphrase a generated question.
5. "Mark one item done and snooze another — however you'd naturally do it."
6. "If you got this app's notification next month, what would make you open it vs swipe it away?"
7. "Would you use this at your next repair visit? Why or why not?" (no leading)
8. Last: "What almost made you give up during any of this?"

Log per person: completion Y/N per task, seconds per task, exact confusion quotes, abandonment point, would-use-next-visit Y/N. Do not average feelings; count behaviors.

## B. 5-Shop Discovery Script (~30 min, service advisors / dealership staff)
Introduce honestly: "I'm building an app that helps customers keep records and prepare questions before service. I'd like to understand how estimates really work. Nothing you say endorses the app."

1. "Walk me through a typical written estimate — what sections does it always have?"
2. "How do you present diagnosis fees? When are they waived?"
3. "How are labor hours and rates shown? Book time or actual?"
4. "How do parts choices get made — OEM, aftermarket, used? How is that written down?"
5. "What extras commonly get added between estimate and final invoice, and why?"
6. "How do customers authorize added work mid-job? Phone? Signature? App?"
7. "What do customers most often misunderstand about estimates?"
8. "What would make a customer who asks questions easier to serve, not harder?"
9. "What paperwork do your best customers bring or keep?"
Never ask: "Do shops upsell?" / "Would you endorse upsell detection?" / anything about fraud.

Output: a real map of US estimate anatomy (sections, vocabulary, authorization flow) to drive Counter Mode line-item types and copy.

## C. Paid organization pilot (cash-flow validation, administrative only)
Offer to beta cohort: "$X flat: we organize your car's paperwork — receipts, dates, mileage evidence, one clean history file, plus a prepared question list for your next visit."
Boundaries: no diagnosis, no legal advice, no safety clearance, no fraud claims, no price-fairness verdicts. Every deliverable mirrors a planned app artifact (timeline, evidence labels, prep sheet) so the work feeds product learning. Stop the pilot if requests drift outside these bounds.
